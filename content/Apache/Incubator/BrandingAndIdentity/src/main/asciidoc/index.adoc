== Branding and Identity in the Apache Incubator

_Guidance for Podlings on Name and Logo Use_

[.notes]
--
Introduce that this session explains how podlings can use Apache names and logos correctly  
and maintain a clear, neutral identity while in incubation.  

Key points:  
* Podlings represent both their own community and the ASF.  
* Correct branding builds trust and avoids confusion.  
* Third parties can use ASF marks if they follow policy,  
  but projects share responsibility for oversight and clarity.  

Encourage participants to think about whether their project’s public presence  
clearly shows it is part of the Apache Incubator.  
--


== Introduction

Incubating projects carry both the Apache name and their own identity.  

Guiding third-party use of the project’s name and logo is an important part of community responsibility.

Misuse can:

* Confuse users  
* Create the impression of ASF endorsement  
* Undermine ASF’s vendor-neutral reputation  

[.notes]
--
Explain that branding is about **clarity, not restriction**.  
Podlings should check how their name and logo appear publicly and step in early if there’s confusion.  
A few simple checks early in incubation prevent bigger issues later.  
--

== Why Third-Party Branding Matters

* Protecting the Apache Name  
* Preventing Misrepresentation  
* Preserving Vendor Neutrality  
* Reducing Graduation Risks  

[.notes]
--
Introduce these four themes as the foundation of the session.  
Each connects to ASF principles: shared stewardship, transparency, and independence.  
You’ll expand on each with real-world examples and good practices.  
--

== Protecting the Apache Name

* Every podling carries the prefix **Apache** 
* Third parties may use ASF marks, but only **when following ASF trademark policy.** 
* Clear and consistent use protects both the podling and the ASF’s reputation.  
* Safeguarding this shared brand is part of being a responsible community.

[.notes]
--
“Apache” is a trademark that represents open governance and community trust.  
It’s fine for others to reference a project name — like “compatible with Apache Foo” — as long as it’s accurate and non-endorsing.  
Encourage podlings to review the ASF trademark policy: https://www.apache.org/foundation/marks/  
Protecting the brand helps maintain ASF’s collective credibility.  
--

== Preventing Misrepresentation

* Third parties may promote software or services as “official” when they are not.  
* Without clarification, users may assume the ASF endorses a product or company.  
* Friendly early conversations help maintain trust and avoid confusion.

[.notes]
--
Misrepresentation is often unintentional.  
Vendors may not understand ASF’s structure or naming requirements.  
Approach corrections gently: thank them for their enthusiasm, explain how ASF works, and share the branding page link.  
Positive engagement builds relationships while protecting integrity.  
--

== Preserving Vendor Neutrality

ASF projects are vendor-neutral. If one company markets a podling as its own product, it can create the impression of ownership.  

This may lead to:

* Less participation from others
* Reduced diversity
* Graduation delays

[.notes]
--
Neutrality supports openness and inclusion.  
A healthy project has visible participation from multiple companies and individuals.  
If the public face appears tied to one company, new contributors may hesitate to join.  
Encourage podlings to share event presence, website updates, and outreach across organizations.  
--

== Reducing Graduation Risks

* Branding and trademark management are part of ASF graduation criteria.
* Projects that handle name and logo use responsibly tend to move through graduation more smoothly.

[.notes]
--
Branding is a checkpoint in the ASF Maturity Model and the Incubator graduation review.  
Demonstrating consistent use of “(incubating)” and proper responses to misuse shows readiness.  
This is about building good habits, not bureaucracy.  
--

== Common Risks (Part 1)

* A vendor markets “Apache Foo Enterprise Edition”, implying ASF endorsement  
* A company displays the ASF logo on its commercial website  
* Blogs or press refer to it as “Company X’s project” 
* Promotional material describes it as “owned” or “developed by Company X”

[.notes]
--
These are frequent issues across the Incubator.  
Such phrasing can confuse the public into thinking a company controls or created the project.  
Podlings should watch how their names appear online and correct issues politely.  
Provide helpful resources like the “Proper Use of Apache Marks” page.  
Tone matters — be collaborative, not confrontational.  
--

== Common Risks (Part 2)

* Only one vendor is visibly active or commercializing the podling  
* The podling omits “(incubating)” on its website or releases  
* One company’s employees dominate governance or mailing lists  
* Third-party materials (whitepapers, training, blogs) present the project as proprietary  
* Event booths or swag show the logo without “(incubating)” or alongside corporate branding

[.notes]
--
These subtle patterns can develop gradually.  
The goal isn’t to assign blame but to restore balance and transparency.  
Encourage diversity by recognizing contributions from multiple organizations.  
Ensure the incubating qualifier appears on all visuals and communications.  
If issues persist, mentors can reach out privately or contact trademarks@apache.org.  
--

== Branding in Incubator Reports

* Quarterly reports invite a short branding update.
* Consider noting:
** Use of the correct name (“Apache Foo (incubating)”)
** Third-party mentions checked and any corrections requested
** Outreach with vendors/events and links to ASF guidance

[.notes]
--
Don’t leave this blank or just write “none.”  
Even “Reviewed public mentions; confirmed consistent use; corrected 2 listings” shows oversight.  
Mentors can remind podlings to keep a small log of branding checks to make quarterly reporting easy.  
--

== The “(Incubating)” Disclaimer

Until graduation, use the full name: **Apache Foo (incubating)**  

Display it on:

* Website, README, docs, and slides  
* Release artifacts and notices  
* Event listings and talk abstracts  

[.notes]
--
This qualifier helps users understand status and governance.  
Third parties should mirror the same form when naming the project.  
Once the Board approves graduation and infra completes renames, the qualifier can be dropped.  
If space is tight (e.g., slides), show it in a footer or sub-title.  
--

== Visual Identity and Logos

Podlings may design logos. Aim to:

* Follow ASF branding guidance  
* Keep project identity distinct from corporate marks  
* Avoid compositions implying endorsement  

[.notes]
--
Logos are strong symbols.  
Combining ASF or podling logos with corporate marks can suggest endorsement.  
If unsure, mentors or trademarks@apache.org can review designs quickly.  
Keep vector originals, color specs, and a grayscale version for consistent use.  
--

== Events, Press, and Mentions

Proactively check how your project appears in:

* Conference schedules and CFP sites  
* Vendor blogs, docs, and whitepapers  
* Press releases and analyst notes  

[.notes]
--
Mislabeling often happens in conference or marketing materials.  
Offer a friendly correction: request “Apache Foo (incubating)” and remove ownership phrasing.  
Provide a concise, correct project description for editors to use.  
--

== Consequences of Not Acting

Unresolved branding issues can:

* Confuse users and contributors  
* Signal weak neutrality  
* Delay graduation reviews  

[.notes]
--
Keep the focus on outcomes, not blame.  
Projects that track and close branding issues early find graduation smoother.  
Establish a simple “notice → ask → fix → record” practice.  
--

== Good Practices for Podlings

* Start early: review name/logo use on all surfaces  
* Monitor public mentions periodically  
* Offer polite, specific corrections with ASF links  
* Summarize progress in quarterly reports  
* Ask mentors or trademarks@apache.org when unsure  

[.notes]
--
Create a light “branding hygiene” routine—maybe once per quarter.  
Rotate the task so it’s shared.  
Thank third parties who make updates; collaboration fosters goodwill.  
--

== Branding and Graduation Readiness

Before graduation, reviewers look for:

* Consistent use of the full name and “(incubating)” qualifier  
* Evidence that the community understands ASF branding principles  
* Balanced, vendor-neutral presentation across project channels  

[.notes]
--
This ties branding directly to graduation success.  
It’s not just compliance—it’s proof of maturity and shared stewardship.  
Remind that graduation reviews often note branding as either a strength or a blocker.  
By demonstrating awareness and balance, a podling shows it’s ready to operate independently as an Apache TLP.  
--

== Getting Help

Unsure about a use case?

* Ask mentors or the IPMC for a quick review  
* Email trademarks@apache.org for assistance  

[.notes]
--
The ASF Trademark team is collaborative and responsive.  
Include URLs and screenshots when asking questions—it speeds up guidance.  
Early questions save time versus fixes during graduation.  
--

== Conclusion

Taking branding seriously from the start helps podlings:

* Provide clear signals to users  
* Demonstrate independence and neutrality  
* Show steady progress toward graduation  

[.notes]
--
Wrap up by linking branding to community health and trust.  
Encourage podlings to make branding checks a normal part of incubation practice.  
Small, steady attention builds long-term credibility and smoother graduation.  
--
