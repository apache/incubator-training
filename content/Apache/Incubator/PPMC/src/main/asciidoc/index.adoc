== Welcome to the PPMC
* Podling Project Management Committee (PPMC)
* Oversees podling during incubation
* Goal: healthy, self-governing community

[.notes]
--
Welcome the new members warmly.  
Explain that being on the PPMC means stepping into both a **leadership and stewardship role**.  
This isn’t only about writing or reviewing code. It’s about helping the podling become a self-sustaining Apache community.  
The PPMC helps the podling grow until it is ready to graduate to a Top-Level Project (TLP).  
--

== The Apache Way
* Transparency
* Meritocracy
* Consensus
* Community over Code

[.notes]
--
Introduce the Apache Way as the **cultural foundation** of the ASF.  
- Transparency: All important decisions happen on public mailing lists.  
- Meritocracy: People gain influence through their contributions.  
- Consensus: Strive for agreement; silence usually means consent.  
- Community over Code: The project’s long-term health is more important than any single technical feature.  
Reinforce that PPMC members are guardians of these principles.  
--

== Your Role
* Active participation
* Guide decisions “the Apache Way”
* Mentor contributors
* Support inclusivity

[.notes]
--
Your role is broader than writing code:  
- Be active in discussions; inactivity weakens the podling.  
- Ensure decisions follow ASF culture, not just personal preference.  
- Help bring in and mentor new contributors so the community grows.  
- Support inclusivity: welcome different viewpoints, time zones, and backgrounds.  
--

== ASF Basics
* ASF is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit
* Podlings are not independent legal entities
* Trademarks and assets owned by the ASF
* PPMC manages the project with the help of the IPMC

[.notes]
--
Clarify the legal structure:  
The ASF is the legal umbrella, a US nonprofit.  
Podlings don’t own their trademarks, domains, or infrastructure, the ASF does.  
The PPMC manages the podling’s community and technical direction but **cannot sign contracts or act as a separate legal entity**.  
This helps avoid legal risk and ensures consistency.  
--

== Committer vs. PPMC
* Initial committers are PPMC members
* Committers: can commit code changes
* PPMC: sets technical direction & community growth

[.notes]
--
Clarify the distinction:  
- Committers have write access to the repository. They review and commit code, but they do not “vote” on patches or PRs.  
- The PPMC, however, looks after the bigger picture: community health, project direction, releases, and oversight.  
Both roles overlap in practice, but PPMC has assumed additional governance responsibilities.  
--

== Relationship with IPMC
* PPMC reports to Incubator PMC
* IPMC oversees incubation
* Some votes (e.g., releases, graduation) need IPMC approval

[.notes]
--
The PPMC is not alone, it works under the supervision of the **Incubator PMC (IPMC)**.  
The IPMC has final oversight during the incubation period.  
Release votes, for example, require approval by both the PPMC *and* the IPMC.  
When the PPMC feels ready for graduation, it proposes it to the IPMC, which decides whether to recommend graduation to the ASF Board.  
--

== Responsibilities
* Review/vote on releases
* Ensure IP/licensing compliance
* Nurture community health
* Engage in discussions

[.notes]
--
The PPMC’s responsibilities include:  
- Reviewing releases to ensure they comply with ASF legal and policy requirements.  
- Checking for license compliance (dependencies must be compatible with ALv2).  
- Supporting and maintaining a healthy, respectful, and diverse community.  
- Being active in discussions, and decisions require participation.  
--

== Voting
* +1, 0, -1
* Lazy consensus common
* PPMC votes are binding

[.notes]
--
Quick primer on voting:  
- +1 = approve, -1 = veto (with reasoning), 0 = neutral.  
- Most ASF decisions use “lazy consensus”, silence is consent.  
- Not all votes are equal: only PPMC members cast **binding votes** in incubation.  
Encourage members to vote, even if it’s just +1, to show engagement.  
--

== Communication
* Use `dev@` mailing list
* Keep decisions public
* Escalate when needed

[.notes]
--
Reinforce that the **mailing list is the official record**.  
Decisions in Slack, Discord, or private chat don’t count unless summarized to the list.  
This ensures transparency, maintains a historical record, and keeps everyone informed.  
If a discussion is stalled or contentious, escalate it early instead of letting it fester.  
--

== Escalation Path
* Start with the PPMC
* Escalate to IPMC if unresolved
* Further escalation: ASF President or Board

[.notes]
--
Explain the chain of escalation:  
1. Try to resolve issues within the PPMC.  
2. If that fails, involve the IPMC, they are responsible for podlings.  
Knowing the path helps avoid confusion when problems arise.  
--

== Releases
* Follow ASF release policy
* Generally 72h minimum vote
* Source = official release
* Distribute via `dist.apache.org`

[.notes]
--
Releases are **the most important PPMC duty**.  
- They must comply with ASF release policy.  
- Voting should run at least 72 hours to give everyone a fair chance.  
- Source is the official release artifact.  
- Binaries are for convenience but must always be built from the source.  
- Final distribution is through ASF’s infrastructure at `dist.apache.org`.  
--

== Diversity of Community
* Broad, diverse participation
* Avoid vendor dominance
* Needed for graduation

[.notes]
--
Graduation requires a diverse community.  
If the project depends on a single vendor or group, it’s at risk.  
The PPMC should actively encourage participation from new people and different organizations.  
This helps ensure long-term sustainability.  
--

== Growth & Graduation
* Add new committers and PPMC members
* Support project diversity
* Graduation depends on community health

[.notes]
--
Graduation is not automatic.  
The PPMC should regularly nominate and vote in new committers and PPMC members.  
A podling that doesn’t grow its community usually won’t graduate.  
Graduation is about proving the project can sustain itself independently as a TLP.  
--

* Join discussions
