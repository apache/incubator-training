== Welcome to the IPMC Community
* Thank you for joining the IPMC
* Your participation strengthens the Incubator
* Even small contributions make a difference

[.notes]
--
This is a collective welcome from the IPMC.
Thank new members for volunteering their time.  
The Incubator only works because IPMC members actively engage — reading reports, voting on releases, giving feedback.  
Even small actions (a quick vote, a short comment on a report) help podlings succeed.  
The message: you are valued, and your involvement matters to the whole Foundation.  
--

== First Steps
* Subscribe to:
** general@incubator.apache.org  
** private@incubator.apache.org
* Review the Incubator Policy
* Familiarize yourself with current podlings and mentors

[.notes]
--
Onboarding starts with connecting to communication channels.  
- The **general@ list** is where most public discussions, votes, and community guidance happen.  
- The **private@ list** is reserved for sensitive matters like conflicts, Code of Conduct issues, or legal concerns.  
Next, review the Incubator Policy so you understand the rules and expectations.  
Finally, take time to look at the current list of podlings and their assigned mentors. This gives you a picture of where oversight and support may be needed.  
--

== IPMC Responsibilities
* Review/vote on releases and graduations
* Monitor podling health and governance
* Help resolve mentor-escalated issues
* Uphold ASF policies

[.notes]
--
The IPMC exists to ensure consistency and quality across all podlings.  
Your main duties are:  
- Reviewing and voting on podling releases and graduation proposals.  
- Monitoring podling health by looking at reports, community activity, and signs of progress.  
- Helping resolve issues that mentors raise when they can’t resolve them within the podling.  
- Acting as a safeguard to ensure ASF legal, branding, and cultural policies are followed.  
Think of yourself as part mentor, part auditor, part cultural ambassador.  
--

== Key Activities
* Join list discussions
* Vote on releases and graduations
* Share observations and raise flags
* Celebrate podling successes

[.notes]
--
It’s not about huge time commitments but about steady presence.  
- Read and occasionally post on general@; even short “+1” or “looks good” replies help.  
- Participate in votes, especially on releases—these need binding IPMC votes to pass.  
- If you see risks, like a single vendor dominating, raise it for discussion.  
- And don’t forget to celebrate progress! Positive feedback helps motivate podlings and reinforces good ASF practices.  
--

== Voting in the IPMC
* Binding votes on releases and graduations
* Lazy consensus is common
* Broad participation is essential

[.notes]
--
Remember: votes are a **record of consensus**, not the process of oversight itself.  
Oversight comes from discussions and careful review; the vote is how we document the outcome and show compliance.  
Votes on releases and graduations are **binding** when cast by IPMC members.  
Lazy consensus means silence is taken as agreement, but please don’t stay silent—visible participation is important for demonstrating active oversight.  
--

== How Oversight Actually Happens
* Mentoring and day-to-day guidance by mentors
* Public discussion on ASF lists (general@, dev@)
* Review of reports, releases, branding/licensing
* Escalation only when needed (PPMC → IPMC → Board)

[.notes]
--
Oversight is primarily cultural and process-driven, not bureaucratic.  
- Mentors are the first line of support, guiding podlings daily.  
- The IPMC monitors discussions and makes sure important matters stay on ASF lists.  
- Reports, releases, and branding checks are practical tools to assess progress.  
- Escalation is a safety net: if the PPMC or mentors can’t resolve something, it comes to the IPMC, and in rare cases further to the ASF President or Board.  
--

== Reviewing Releases
Check for:
* LICENSE and NOTICE files
* Dependency license compliance
* Correct process (vote on dev@, 72h minimum)
* Branding and naming compliance
* No compiled code in source

[.notes]
--
Releases are where the IPMC’s role is most visible.  
Things to check:  
- Are the LICENSE and NOTICE files present and correct?  
- Do all dependencies have ASF-compatible licenses?  
- Was the vote run properly on the dev@ list, with at least 72 hours?  
- Is branding correct (e.g., “Apache Foo (incubating)”), and no misuse of ASF trademarks?  
- Source must be authoritative: no compiled code should sneak into the source release.  
Mentors may catch many issues, but a second set of eyes is critical.  
--

== Graduation Criteria
Podlings must show:
* A sustainable, diverse community
* ASF infrastructure and process use
* Transparent, merit-based governance
* At least one ASF release without mentor intervention
* Licensing and branding compliance

[.notes]
--
Graduation isn’t automatic—it’s a judgment call by the IPMC and Board.  
Key signals:  
- The community is active and not dominated by one vendor.  
- They’ve switched to ASF infrastructure (lists, repos, website).  
- Governance is transparent and merit-based: new committers and PPMC members are added openly.  
- They’ve produced at least one proper ASF release, ideally more.  
- Licensing and branding issues have been addressed.  
The Maturity Model can be a helpful checklist, but human judgment is always required.  
--

== Reviewing Graduation Proposals
Look for:
* Community diversity and independence
* Recent ASF-compliant releases
* Mailing list engagement and transparency
* Signs of self-governance
* Questions or concerns — raise them!

[.notes]
--
Graduation reviews are critical — they decide if a podling becomes a Top-Level Project.  
When reviewing, check:  
- **Diversity**: Is the community broad, or dominated by one vendor?  
- **Releases**: Have they done at least one proper ASF release without heavy mentor intervention?  
- **Mailing list activity**: Are discussions happening openly and consistently?  
- **Self-governance**: Is the PPMC leading, not just mentors or one company?  
Don’t be afraid to ask questions. A graduation vote is a good moment to double-check readiness.  
Your feedback helps the Board and the community feel confident about graduation.  
--

== Monitoring Podling Health
Watch for:
* Stalled communication
* No release progress
* Single-vendor dominance
* Weak mentorship
* Community stagnation

[.notes]
--
Podling health issues usually show up in predictable ways:  
- If mailing list traffic drops or questions go unanswered, that’s a red flag.  
- A podling that doesn’t produce releases may never prove it can operate as an ASF project.  
- If one company controls all the committers, it threatens long-term sustainability.  
- If mentors are silent, podlings lose vital guidance.  
- Stagnant or shrinking communities rarely graduate.  
The earlier the IPMC notices and raises these concerns, the better chance the podling has to fix them.  
--

== Communication & Conduct
* Use ASF mailing lists for decisions
* Avoid private channels
* Model respectful, inclusive behavior
* Escalate problems if needed

[.notes]
--
Communication at ASF is about transparency and respect.  
- All important project decisions must be on ASF mailing lists, not Slack or private chats.  
- Keep discussions inclusive—welcome contributions from different backgrounds and time zones.  
- If you see abusive or disruptive behavior, escalate it. Mentors can start, but the IPMC may need to step in.  
- Remember that your tone sets an example. As IPMC members, we’re modeling ASF culture.  
--

== Common Pitfalls
* Overly passive mentors
* Podlings “parking” in the Incubator
* Releases with licensing issues
* Vendor dominance limiting community growth
* Off-list or private decision-making

[.notes]
--
The IPMC should be alert to recurring traps:  
- Mentors who never engage or sign off weaken incubation.  
- Some podlings get stuck in the Incubator for years without progress—sometimes due to low community activity.  
- Release licensing problems are the #1 technical blocker; catching them early helps.  
- Vendor dominance is a cultural blocker: a podling dominated by one company risks becoming a pseudo-product, not an ASF project.  
- Off-list decisions (on Slack, GitHub, or private email) violate ASF transparency and must be redirected to mailing lists.  
--

== Conflict of Interest
* Step back when conflicts arise
* Be transparent
* Ask on private@ if unsure

[.notes]
--
Conflicts of interest aren’t bad if handled properly.  
Examples: voting on a podling release where your employer has a stake, or discussing a community where you’re directly involved.  
If you have a conflict, declare it and step back. Transparency maintains trust.  
If you’re not sure whether something counts, ask privately on private@ and get advice.  
--

== Time Commitment
* Expect a few hours per month
* Focus on release/graduation votes
* Participate in discussions
* More involvement always welcome

[.notes]
--
The time requirement is modest: a couple of hours per month.  
At minimum, read the mailing lists, vote on a few releases, and weigh in on graduation proposals.  
But the more you contribute, the healthier the Incubator becomes. Even small, steady contributions go a long way.  
--

== Stepping Down
* Membership is voluntary
* Notify private@ if you want to step back
* Better to go inactive formally than silently
* You can rejoin later

[.notes]
--
Membership is not permanent or obligatory. If you no longer have time, step down formally.  
It’s far better than silently disappearing. This way, the active IPMC list stays accurate.  
You can always rejoin later when you have the time or interest again.  
--
