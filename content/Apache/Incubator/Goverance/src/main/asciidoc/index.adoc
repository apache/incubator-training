== Governance in Practice
_Turning ASF Principles into Everyday Project Practice_

[.notes]
--
Introduce the topic by framing governance as a journey — not a rulebook.  
Explain that the goal of incubation is to help podlings learn how to govern themselves,  
moving from mentor-guided decision-making to independent, community-driven leadership.  
--

== Purpose
This guide helps podlings apply ASF governance principles as they progress along the Incubation path.

* Explains what governance looks like day-to-day  
* Describes governance at key milestones  
* Assumes a “typical” podling, variations are normal

[.notes]
--
Governance isn’t just about policy, it’s about culture and daily habits.  
Each podling develops its own rhythm, but all must show progress toward ASF-style transparency and community-led decision-making.  
This session explains what that looks like in practice and how mentors can help guide the process.
--

## Core Principles in Practice
* **Community over Code** - Decisions made through open consensus.  
* **Trust through Contribution** - Responsibility grows with demonstrated participation and collaboration.  
* **Transparency** - All decisions visible on public mailing lists.  
* **Independence** - Projects can thrive beyond any one employer.  

[.notes]
--
These four principles are the foundation of ASF governance.  
They are interdependent: transparency builds trust, trust supports independence, and community-driven decisions keep projects healthy long term.  
Podlings are expected to practice and demonstrate all four before graduation.  
--

== Decision-Making at the ASF
* **Consensus** - Discuss until broad agreement is reached  
* **Lazy consensus** - Silence implies consent for routine actions  
* **[VOTE] threads** - Used for binding decisions (e.g., new committers, releases)  
* **At least three +1s** required for release approval  
* Handle disagreements through discussion, not majority rule

[.notes]
--
ASF decision-making focuses on *discussion first, voting second.*  
Most podling actions are handled through lazy consensus, only raise a formal vote when needed for releases or new committers.  
Strictly speaking, only PMC votes are binding at the ASF level.  
However, during incubation, the PPMC uses voting to practice governance under mentor oversight, building readiness for eventual PMC responsibility.  
When disagreements arise, avoid rushing to a vote, instead, seek consensus and understanding.
--

== Early Stage (0–3 months)
* Establish `dev@` as the public decision center  
* Form the **PPMC** with mentors and initial committers  
* Practice **lazy consensus** and transparent communication  
* Mentors model ASF process and redirect private discussions to the list

[.notes]
--
Early months define the podling’s communication culture.  
All decisions should happen on `dev@` to ensure transparency.  
Mentors demonstrate good habits, for example, moving decisions out of private or corporate channels and explaining why this matters.  
This helps set expectations and fosters inclusivity from the beginning.
--

== Growth Stage (3–12 months)
* Add new **committers** based on merit and trust  
* Expand the **PPMC** with active, responsible contributors  
* Encourage **multi-company diversity**  
* Produce the **first ASF release** (~6 months target)  
* Submit **timely podling reports**  
* Record key decisions publicly (wiki or list archive)

[.notes]
--
This stage shows whether the project is learning to self-govern.  
Adding committers demonstrates recognition of merit.  
Expanding the PPMC distributes oversight and prepares for independence.  
The first release validates that the community understands ASF policy and can collaborate effectively.  
Regular reporting builds accountability and gives the IPMC confidence that the project is progressing.
--

== Reporting as a Governance Tool
* Reports reviewed by the **IPMC** for oversight  
* Demonstrate transparency and community health  
* Use reports to:
** Reflect on progress and blockers  
** Ask for help or mentor input  
** Record public accountability

[.notes]
--
Reporting is not busywork, it’s one of the most powerful governance tools.  
It allows the podling to reflect on what’s working, share challenges, and keep mentors and the IPMC informed.  
Encourage podlings to draft reports collectively; this helps them own their progress and practice self-review.
--

== Maturing Stage (12+ months)
* **PPMC leads** governance and oversight independently  
* **Mentors step back** into advisory roles  
* **Diversity:** no single company dominates committers or PPMC  
* Sustainable governance means continuity through turnover  
* Demonstrate:
** Sustainable, balanced community  
** Regular ASF-compliant releases  
** Mailing list as the decision hub

[.notes]
--
By this stage, mentors should only need to provide guidance or context, not drive decisions.  
A mature PPMC runs its own votes, manages releases, and leads community growth.  
Independence becomes visible through balanced participation across companies and consistent self-management.  
True sustainability means the project continues smoothly even when individuals or companies change, a hallmark of ASF projects.
--

== Common Governance Pitfalls
* Private decision-making (Slack, WeChat, or company email)  
* Over-reliance on one vendor or employer  
* Mentors making or approving all key decisions  
* Failing to resolve disagreements through discussion  
* Releases without community involvement or recorded votes

[.notes]
--
These are the most common warning signs that governance needs improvement.  
Private decisions and company dominance undermine trust and ASF values.  
Mentor-led votes delay growth and can make graduation harder.  
Release transparency and open disagreement resolution are core to ASF maturity, always bring conversations back to the public list.
--

== Checklist at Each Milestone
* **3 months:** dev@ active? PPMC formed?  
* **6 months:** First release? New committer added?  
* **12 months:** Expanded PPMC? Self-governance evident?  
* **Before graduation:**  
** Diverse, independent, transparent community  
** Regular ASF releases  
** Consensus on graduation resolution

[.notes]
--
These checkpoints provide a simple roadmap for podlings and mentors.  
Exact timing may differ, but steady movement toward these indicators is key.  
The final step, drafting and gaining consensus on a graduation resolution, confirms the community is ready for independence.
--

== Reflection Questions
* Are all major decisions visible on the mailing list?  
* Does the PPMC lead governance or rely on mentors?  
* Are contributors recognized promptly through committership?  
* Could the project continue if the sponsor company stepped back?

[.notes]
--
These questions help projects self-assess beyond metrics.  
They probe community resilience, independence, and fairness.  
If any answer raises concerns, it’s an opportunity for mentoring and cultural adjustment, not a failure.  
Self-awareness and reflection are marks of a healthy ASF community.
--

## Summary
* ASF governance matures through practice.  
* Mentors guide, PPMC leads.  
* Transparency and inclusiveness are essential.  
* Healthy podlings govern themselves, *the Apache Way*.  
* Graduation isn’t a checklist. It’s recognition that the community already operates as an ASF project.  

[.notes]
--
Wrap up by emphasizing that governance is learned through doing, not documentation.  
Mentors succeed when they become nearly unnecessary.  
Graduation happens when the PPMC already behaves like an ASF PMC, transparent, trusted, and self-sustaining.  
That’s what defines a lasting and resilient Apache community.
